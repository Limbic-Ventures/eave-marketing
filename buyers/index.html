The ground and the county

What the listing
never says.

Every value shows where it came from and when. Estimates say estimate. A blank shows a dash, never a zero. Eave shows the official zone and links the model; it never presents a vendor’s score as a fact, and it never rates a school or a neighbourhood.

The things buyers actually ask

Questions, answered.

I haven’t picked a house yet. Is this for me?

Yes. That is exactly when it helps. Line up the homes you are considering and Eave answers the same questions about each of them: what the ground and the county say, what the documents say, and what it will roughly cost to bring the house up.

Do I need my agent to use Eave?

No. If your agent runs their buyers in Eave, they will invite you and your search is waiting when you sign in. If they don’t, start one yourself.

Where do the flood, fire, and tax numbers come from?

Official zone maps and county records, each labelled with its source and date. Eave shows the official designation and links the model behind it. It never presents a vendor’s risk score as a fact, and an estimate always says estimate.

Is the cost estimate an inspection?

No. It is a range from photos and documents, with the evidence shown beside it. Rough ranges from photos and documents. Not an inspection. Not an appraisal. Get a licensed inspection before you rely on a number to make an offer.

What happens to the seller’s documents?

They stay on the house they are about. Eave reads a document only when you ask, keeps every original, and shows what changed when a newer version arrives. Your notes are private unless you share one, and the search is yours at close. How documents are handled →
