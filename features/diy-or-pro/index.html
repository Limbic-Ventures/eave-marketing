The weekend question

Can I do this myself,
or do I call someone?

A dripping faucet, a sagging fence, a water heater on its last year. The honest answer depends on the job, the tools you already own, how long it takes, whether it needs a permit, and what your Saturday is worth.

DIY or pro puts those pieces side by side on every project, so you choose with the numbers in front of you, and change your mind without starting over.

Three paths

How will you handle this?

The same three choices appear on every project, from a faucet swap to a new roof. Nothing locks you in: start as DIY, and switch to Hire it out if the job turns out bigger than it looked.

01 · DIY

I’ll do it myself

A materials estimate for the exact job, grouped the way you’ll shop for it.

  • Each item with a low and high price
  • Difficulty: Easy, Moderate, Advanced, or Professional Only
  • Hours, a permit flag, and what the tool rental costs
  • Items you already bought, marked “have from history” from past purchases

Materials estimate · Intelligence

02 · Learn & do

Guide me through it

For the job you’d like to do yourself but haven’t done before.

  • YouTube and written-guide searches for the job
  • “Find the best ones for this job”: three tutorials, each with its source, length, and why
  • A “Watch out for” list of common mistakes
  • A “Start here” line with the first step

Free on every plan

03 · Hire it out

Find a contractor

A three-step tracker from the first search to the pro you pick.

  • Find local pros for the trade, add someone you know, or pick from your contractors
  • Getting quotes: enter each amount and attach the quote
  • Pick the winner: saved to your contractors, and the project set to hire
  • Eave doesn’t contact pros or book them for you

Trade search free · project matches on Intelligence

Hire it out

Your contractors, and the ones you haven’t met yet

Contractors has two tabs. My contractors is your own contact book: add a pro by form, scan a business card, paste a contact, or paste a Thumbtack, Angi, Yelp, or Houzz profile link. They carry across every home on your account.

Find local pros searches by trade, free. With Eave Intelligence, a project gets its own matches, each with a line on why it’s worth hiring out. A “Find a pro” chip sits on tasks too.

Budgets

Every row, priced both ways

Each project in Budgets has a DIY and a PRO toggle beside its cost. Flip a row to PRO and the budget follows, so the plan can hire out the water heater and still have you building the shelves.

The DIY vs hire calculator puts a number on the choice. Enter what an hour of your time is worth and the estimated hours, and it sets doing it yourself against hiring it out.

What you already own

Start from the garage, not the store

Tools & Materials keeps your tools, kits, outdoor power equipment, and project stock in one list. Tool-kit checklists turn what’s missing into a shopping list.

When a project’s plan lists tools and materials, the ones you’ve bought before are marked as ones you likely have, read from your purchase history, so the list you take to the store is shorter.

What it won’t do. Eave helps you choose and keeps track; it doesn’t hire anyone. It won’t contact a pro, request bids, or book a job, and it takes no cut. Questions, answered

Common questions.

Does Eave book contractors or ask for quotes for me?

No. Eave is your quote tracker, not a marketplace. It helps you find local pros, then you contact them, enter each quote as it comes in, attach it, and pick the winner. It takes no commission. More on contractors →

What’s free and what needs Intelligence?

Free: the three paths on every project, Learn & do, your contractor list, the trade-based local pro search, Budgets with the DIY or PRO toggle, and Tools & Materials. Eave Intelligence, at $2.99 a month or $24 a year, adds the materials estimate for DIY and project-specific pro matches. See pricing →

Can I switch paths partway through?

Yes. The choice is yours to change. Start as DIY, and if the job turns out bigger than it looked, switch to Hire it out and start tracking quotes.

Does Learn & do include how-to videos?

It finds them for you. You get YouTube and written-guide searches for the job, three tutorials picked for it with the reason for each, a list of common mistakes to watch for, and the first step. The tutorials themselves live on the sites that made them.

How do I add a contractor I already use?

Under Contractors, fill in the form, scan their business card, paste their contact details, or paste a Thumbtack, Angi, Yelp, or Houzz profile link. Your contractors carry across every home on your account.

How accurate are the cost estimates?

They’re ranges to plan with, not quotes. The materials estimate shows a low and high price per item, and guided plans are stamped with the date their live data was pulled. Check prices at your store, and get quotes from pros before you hire.
