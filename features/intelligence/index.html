The order problem

Everything a house needs is on a list somewhere.
Nobody tells you the order.

The gutters, the furnace filter, the water heater nobody has flushed, the warranty that runs out next month, the project that has been waiting on a quote since August. None of it is hard to find. The hard part is knowing which one matters this week, and which can wait until spring.

Plans and priorities reads your home’s record and puts it in order: one number for how the house is doing, a short list for this week, the prep for this season, and a plan for the year.

Layer one · the Upkeep Score

One number for how
the house is doing.

The Upkeep Score runs from 0 to 100 and is built from three parts. It rewards the work that keeps a house healthy, not the number of tasks you add. Consistency is shown on the card, but it doesn’t move the number.

Grades run from Neglected, At Risk, and Fair to Well-Maintained, Excellent, and Model Home. The card on Home carries a hint like “Clearing stalled projects moves this the most,” shows how many points you gained this week, and shows badges as you earn them. How the score works, in detail →

45%
Maintenance: seasonal tasks done on time. Only seasons that have finished count.
30%
Backlog health: projects resolved, against projects left to age.
25%
Preventive care: work done ahead of time, against emergencies. An emergency counts double.

Layer two · this week

Three things,
and why each one.

Home opens on this week’s short list. Eave ranks up to five items and shows the top three, each with Done and Undo, and a line saying why it made the cut. When nothing is due, it says so: “Nothing needs you this week.”

Home · this week

Ranked by rules you can read

The list is built the same way every time. Overdue safety tasks come first, then other overdue tasks, then warranties ending within 30 days. After that: this season’s tasks and warranties ending within 90 days, projects waiting or on hold, and high-priority projects you haven’t started.

Because it follows rules rather than a model, it’s free on every plan, and the reason on each item is the rule that put it there.

Home · the one thing

If you only have time for one thing

Above the list, one card picks a single item and says why: the gutters and the water heater flush are both past due, so start with the gutters, the one with a deadline. A button takes you straight to it, and “Why” shows the reasoning.

Add a life event, like a listing date or a renovation, and the list re-ranks around it. Related items move up and say so: “Timed with” the event.

Tasks · season ahead

The season, before it turns

On Tasks, Season ahead lists the prep the coming season asks for, timed to the first and last frost dates for your address. It counts the items, “that’s all fall asks of you,” and gives a rough cost for the season.

The rest of the year waits below, under “Later — handled when it’s time,” with the dates coming up. Tasks has its own punch list, filtered by all, overdue, due soon, and done. Send your upkeep tasks to Google Calendar, or download them for any calendar app.

Layer three · the year, with Eave Intelligence

Twelve months,
each with a reason.

Eave Intelligence is the upgrade that turns the record into a plan for the year: $2.99 a month or $24 a year. The score, this week, and Season ahead stay free.

Upkeep Plan

A plan for every month of the year

Press Generate my upkeep plan and Eave builds twelve month cards. Each has a focus line, like “Fall prep — gutters, sprinkler blowout, furnace tune-up before first frost,” and two to five tasks.

Every task shows its priority, cost, and hours, a DIY or Hire out tag, and a “why now” line. Where Eave knows the maker’s schedule for your model, the plan uses it. Totals sit on top: the annual estimate, how many tasks are high priority, and the total count. Rebuild plan replaces the current 12-month schedule with a new one.

Questions, answered

Common questions.

What’s free, and what comes with Eave Intelligence?

The Upkeep Score, the This week list with its one thing, Season ahead on Tasks, reminders, and your home’s risk profile are free for one home. Eave Intelligence, at $2.99 a month or $24 a year, adds the 12-month Upkeep Plan, the AI Project Queue, and Equipment Watch. Free vs Intelligence →

How is the Upkeep Score calculated?

It’s a 0 to 100 score from three parts: Maintenance at 45% (seasonal tasks done on time, counting only finished seasons), Backlog health at 30% (projects resolved against projects left to age), and Preventive care at 25% (work done ahead of time against emergencies, which count double). Your Upkeep Score, explained →

How does Eave decide what goes on This week?

By fixed rules. Overdue safety tasks first, then other overdue tasks, warranties ending within 30 days, this season’s tasks and warranties ending within 90 days, projects waiting or on hold, and high-priority projects not started. It ranks up to five and shows the top three, each with its reason.

What’s in the 12-month Upkeep Plan?

Twelve month cards, each with a focus line and two to five tasks. Every task has a priority, cost, hours, a DIY or Hire out tag, and a “why now” line, using the maker’s schedule for your model where Eave knows it. The totals show the annual estimate, the high-priority count, and the total tasks. Your 12-month care plan →

What happens when I’m about to sell or renovate?

Add it as a life event. Items related to an upcoming listing or renovation move up the This week list and are marked “Timed with” that event. Selling? Eave for Sellers →

How do reminders work?

Send your upkeep tasks to Google Calendar as recurring all-day events, or download an .ics file for Apple Calendar or any other calendar. Reminders on individual tasks will arrive through the Eave mobile apps, which are coming soon.

Does this work for a business or nonprofit?

Yes. Commercial Upkeep uses the same score, ranked list, and plan for an organization’s properties, and members of a paying organization get the Intelligence features. Commercial Upkeep →
