The model problem

Someone else owns
your exact water heater.

And they’ve had the same questions you will: how often to flush it, which parts fit, what tends to go wrong as it ages. The maker wrote most of the answers down. Owners filled in the rest. Each of you has had to go and find them alone.

A knowledge graph is a set of facts joined to the things they’re about. In Eave’s product knowledge graph, each thing is one brand and model, and the facts are what’s been published about it: the maintenance schedule, the known issues, the parts, and the documents. The appliance on your home knowledge graph points to its model’s profile.

Look a model up once, and everyone who owns it has the answer.

Research one appliance

The details for the unit in your kitchen

Open any appliance and ask Eave to research it. It searches the web for that unit and brings back its typical lifespan, the warranty terms, the filter it takes, a rough replacement cost, any recall notices it finds, and a set of suggested reminders with how often each is due.

Nothing lands on your appliance until you apply it. No profile for your model yet? With Eave Intelligence, Look up manufacturer info builds one for the library, so the next owner of the same model reads it for free.

Suggested upkeep

Your model’s tasks come first

Under each appliance and system, Suggested upkeep lists the jobs worth putting on the calendar. When the profile has the maker’s schedule for your model, those tasks rank above the generic ones that fit any furnace or any dishwasher.

Tick the ones you want and add them to your plan in one tap. The rest stay as suggestions.

Equipment Watch

Known issues, matched to your unit’s age

A problem that tends to show up late in a model’s life doesn’t need you in its first year. Equipment Watch, in your Upkeep Plan, lists the known issues that match each appliance’s current age, so the ones in front of you are the ones that apply now.

When Eave lays out your 12-month plan, it uses the manufacturer’s schedule wherever it knows it. The Upkeep Plan and Equipment Watch are part of Eave Intelligence.

One shared library

Looked up once.
Read by every owner.

Your home’s record is yours alone. The product knowledge graph is the opposite: one library that every Eave user reads from, about models rather than people.

01

One profile per model

Every owner of the same brand and model reads the same profile. Profiles match model families too, so a close relative of your model can still find its match.

02

Built as owners look things up

The library grows one lookup at a time. When someone researches a model nobody has looked up yet, the result is saved to that model’s profile.

03

Free to read, on every plan

Reading a profile that already exists costs nothing, Free plan included. Only a new lookup is part of Eave Intelligence.

04

Nothing personal in it

A profile holds no personal data: nothing about who owns the model or where it’s installed. Your appliance’s own record stays private to your home.

05

A source on every task

Each maintenance task links to where it came from, and each section of the profile shows how confident Eave is in it.

06

Works beside your own manual

Upload the manual that came in the box and Eave reads it into your appliance’s record as well: specs, upkeep tasks, and how often they’re due.

HOW MANUALS ARE READ →
What it won’t do. A profile is research, not a service visit. It won’t inspect your unit, diagnose a fault, or tell you a repair is safe to do yourself. Recalls come from two places outside the profile: the research on a single appliance flags any recall notice it finds, and every week Eave checks your appliances against the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission’s recall database, SaferProducts.gov, by brand and model.

Questions, answered

Common questions.

What is a product knowledge graph?

A shared library of what’s known about specific models: one profile per brand and model, with the manufacturer’s maintenance schedule, known issues by age, common parts with part numbers, and documents. Your appliance on your home knowledge graph points to its model’s profile.

Is it free?

Reading a profile that already exists is free on every plan. Look up manufacturer info, which researches a model that isn’t in the library yet, is part of Eave Intelligence at $2.99 a month or $24 a year, along with Equipment Watch in the Upkeep Plan. See pricing →

Does anything about me or my home go into a profile?

No. A profile is about the model and holds no personal data. Your appliance’s own record, with its serial number, install date, and history, stays private to your home. Data & privacy →

How accurate is it?

Profiles are researched from manufacturer and community sources. Every maintenance task links to its source, and each section of a profile shows how confident Eave is in it. Check it against your own manual, and when they disagree, go with the manual. Uploading manuals →

What if my model isn’t in the library?

Profiles match model families as well as exact models, so a close relative may already be there. If not, Look up manufacturer info builds the profile, and it stays in the library for the next owner of that model. Systems and appliances →

How does it change my upkeep?

In Suggested upkeep, tasks from your model’s profile rank above generic ones. Your Upkeep Plan uses the manufacturer’s schedule where it knows it, and Equipment Watch lists the known issues that match each appliance’s current age. Plans and priorities →

Does a profile include recalls?

No. Recalls come from two places outside the profile: the research on a single appliance flags any recall notice it finds, and every week Eave checks your appliances against the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission’s recall database, SaferProducts.gov, by brand and model.
